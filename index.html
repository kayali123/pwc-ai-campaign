When AI says scale, who says wait?

I led a five-person UCD team to propose a B2B campaign for PwC's AI advisory business. Our idea: show the human judgment behind an AI decision, then connect that proof to a qualified conversation with a prospective client.

01 · The brief

A credible promise needed a buying reason.

PwC already described its approach as “human-led, tech-powered.” For a company weighing an AI advisory partner, the missing piece was a concrete view of what the people would do when the technology gave an incomplete answer.

Our proposal used a documentary format to make that judgment visible. The business objective was to help relevant enterprise buyers take a next step with PwC, not simply to earn views.

The question we answered

How can a global brand line become evidence that an enterprise buyer can discuss with colleagues and take into a first advisory meeting?

PwC used the “human-led, tech-powered” line in its 2024 AI communications.

For a live pilot, these buying-group questions would be tested with the selected market and service line.

01 / BUSINESS SPONSOR

Will this create value?

Needs a clear use case, a credible path from pilot to work, and a partner who can make a decision.

02 / TECHNOLOGY LEAD

Where might it fail?

Needs to see testing, exceptions, human review and the limits of the system.

03 / RISK & PROCUREMENT

Can we explain the choice?

Needs a documented process and confidence in the people responsible for it.

A sharper competitive claim

Putting people in an AI campaign was already familiar. EY's 2023 “Face of the Future” campaign featured more than 200 employees. Our proposed difference was to show what experts decide when an AI answer needs checking. See EY's announcement ↗

Cross-cultural translation

Keep the global promise; choose local proof. A live pilot would select one EU market, one service line and target accounts with PwC's sales team, then adapt language, industry examples and the buying group. “Europe” is a context for the proposal, not one uniform buyer.

02 · The creative idea

Film the moment an expert says “wait.”

The 2024 proposal was a documentary-led campaign. Instead of a polished claim about responsible AI, viewers would watch consultants question an answer, test its limits and explain the decision that follows.

The story gains tension from an apparently good recommendation that turns out to need a closer look. Expert judgment is the plot.

4 · The decision

The plan changes.

They recommend a narrower rollout, human review and a clear escalation point.

Illustrative storyboard created for this portfolio page.

“Human-led” becomes an action.

The expert questions the model, checks the edge cases and owns the recommendation. That is more useful to a buyer than another portrait of a confident consultant.

“Tech-powered” still matters.

The AI result starts the story. Showing where it helps and where human review changes the decision makes the proposition more credible.

03 · Business path

From a scene worth watching to a reason to meet.

The film does one job: make PwC's method legible. This portfolio plan develops the idea into four proposed episodes and about twenty channel cuts over 16 weeks. A live pilot would release them in stages, with the series open to view.

Each channel then moves a relevant buyer toward a practical conversation. PR supports credibility; sales qualifies whether the attention has become a real AI advisory discussion.

01 / NOTICE

Short scene

A 15–30 second LinkedIn cut shows the precise moment the consultant questions the model. Target named accounts and relevant roles.

Next → Watch the decision
02 / UNDERSTAND

A public episode follows the full problem, check and decision. Industry PR and a small screening add third-party attention.

Next → Apply the question

Next → Speak to an adviser
04 / CONVERSE

Qualified meeting

Sales follows up when a relevant account has a defined AI workstream and a reason to discuss PwC's approach.

Next → Scope an opportunity

16 weeks, with distinct jobs

Weeks 1–3Validate & tease

Test whether buyers understand the scene; clear people, material and claims before release.

Weeks 4–6Launch

Publish the first episode, seed a trade story and concentrate account-targeted distribution.

Weeks 7–12Deepen

Release approved follow-ups and channel-native cuts. Learn which roles engage and what they ask.

Weeks 13–16Convert & review

Offer the discussion guide, hand relevant interest to sales and review account-level signals.

04 · Budget logic

Spend to make the proof. Release the rest when it works.

For this portfolio exercise, I use a €420k single-market pilot scenario. The allocation below revises my earlier planning assumptions for a possible live test. The largest line pays for the evidence on screen; distribution then brings it to the right accounts.

The key refinement is a release rule: validate buyer response and brand safety before committing to the full series.

Gate 1 · Before the full series

Can the audience explain the value?

Use the €30k research line and a €55k first cut within production. Proceed only if target buyers understand the human check, perceived expertise holds, and PwC can clear the story and materials.

Gate 2 · Before heavier media

Does it create useful demand?

Release the remaining production and distribution in stages. Review target-account engagement and the questions sales hears; change the cut or stop scaling if attention does not turn into credible conversations.

05 · My role & outcome

The concept had to work in a cautious room.

I originated the documentary idea and led a five-person team. I brought the creative, channel plan, budget and measurement into one English-language proposal for PwC's EU marketing team.

The creative boundary matters: show the check and decision, use cleared material, and put consent and review into production. That preserves the tension without treating client confidentiality as an afterthought.

What actually happened in 2024

Presented to PwC. Highest mark.

The team completed the academic proposal, presented it to PwC's EU marketing team and received the highest mark in the UCD programme. The campaign itself was not commissioned or launched, so this case does not claim commercial performance.

What this case shows

Translation across contexts.

I took a global English-language brand promise and asked what evidence a European enterprise buying group would need to discuss it seriously. I also had to make the creative case in an English client presentation while balancing brand, risk and commercial goals.

2026 portfolio reflection · separate from the original pitch

Before a live launch, I would select one EU market with PwC's commercial team, test the first episode before producing the full series, and agree on sales-accepted meeting criteria. Market adoption differs substantially across EU countries, and AI rules have changed since 2024. Those checks would precede any new claim or local rollout.
